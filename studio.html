You are here: the workshop

The Studio

This is a making class. You pick a real arts organization, study it all semester, and build work you can show a future boss.

Less cramming. More making.

Starts with the fundamentals. Curiosity mandatory.

What you’ll walk out with

  1. The fundamentals, for real

    Product, price, place, promotion. You’ll know the marketing mix well enough to explain it at a party, and to use it on Monday.

  2. A playbook built for the arts

    Selling a season is not selling sneakers. You’ll develop best practices that fit museums, theaters, orchestras, and the scrappy collective down the street.

  3. A feel for arts audiences

    Who shows up, who doesn’t, and why. You’ll learn to describe the people in the seats, and the ones you wish were there, with real nuance.

  4. Sharp analysis skills

    You’ll size up the competition and run a SWOT analysis that leads somewhere: a marketing plan with a point of view.

  5. Research you can trust

    You’ll practice the research and evaluation methods that let you say “here’s what the data shows” and mean it.

On the workbench

What you’ll make

Your own website

You are a brand too. You’ll build a personal site and keep sharpening it as your marketing chops grow.

A public voice

LinkedIn posts and journal entries that get you thinking out loud and start building your arts management profile.

Three deep dives

Product and brand. Place, price, and positioning. Audience engagement and a full SWOT. All on the organization you chose.

A communications plan

The finale. Everything you’ve learned, turned into a plan your organization could pick up and run with.

From first look to final plan

The arc

  1. Early on

    Choose your organization

    A museum, a theater, a dance company, a festival. Pick one you’re curious about. You’ll get to know it very well.

  2. All semester

    Build your own brand alongside it

    Your website and your posts grow as the course does. What you learn on Thursday shows up in your work by the weekend.

  3. The middle stretch

    Take it apart, piece by piece

    What does it offer? Where, and for how much? Who comes, who doesn’t, and what’s standing in the way?

  4. Exam week

    Put it back together, better

    Your communications plan is the capstone. No blue book. A plan.
